Pico — the dot proposal

The dot is the resting state of the whole feature: always present while Pico is on, click-through, never focusable. Colour is the only thing that changes, so the user never has to look away to know what the app is doing.

Idle — nothing under the pointer takes text. Dim, low contrast, visible on both light and dark desktops.
Armed — the probe says this point is an editable target. Warm, with a halo. This is the “you may double-click” cue.
Listening — recording. The dot is about to become the panel.
Transcribing — audio closed, waiting for words.
Delivering — text going in. Brief, so it reads as a confirmation.
Blocked — elevated window, secure desktop or fullscreen. Nothing is drawn at all; a dot that lies is worse than no dot.

Pico — the panel proposal

The capsule keeps its content but loses its fixed home. It opens next to the dot and closes back into it. Two ways to join them — pick one.

Ship the release notes and
Variant A keeps the dot as the anchor and grows the panel to its right. The dot stays visible as the “still listening” light, and the panel can be any width without the dot moving.
Variant B dissolves the dot into the panel’s left cap: one object, no seam, but the panel must then carry the live-dot colour itself.
Placement rule — the same clamp as the dot: below-right of the pointer, pulled back inside the work area near a screen edge, mirrored to the left when there is no room on the right.

The morph proposal

Dot and panel must never be on screen in the same place twice. The dot is the resting form; the panel is the working form.

idle → armed → listening → panel → transcribing → panel closes → dot
Opening — 180–240 ms, ease-out. The panel should feel like it unfolds from the dot, not like a second window appeared.
Closing — faster, ~140 ms. Hiding late is what makes an overlay feel sticky.
Reduced motion — no scale, no width animation; swap instantly.
